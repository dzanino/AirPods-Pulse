FAQ

Frequently asked questions

I hear nothing

Monitoring is muted until headphones are connected (feedback protection). Connect AirPods or headphones and check the “Hear the probe in headphones” switch. Press the probe more firmly onto bare skin – automatic gain (the AUTO label by the slider) makes up for a weak signal.

The sound suddenly turns mono and “phone-like”

That is the Bluetooth hands-free profile: when the app uses the AirPods microphone, older AirPods (without the H2 chip) also switch playback to 16 kHz mono. Fix: use the iPhone probe mode, or AirPods Pro 2/3 or AirPods 4 on iOS 26.

The sound in my ear sometimes ducks

Check Settings › Bluetooth › AirPods: turn off Conversation Awareness and Adaptive mode, keep Noise Cancellation. On a hands-free link the app turns off its own ducking during probe movement automatically.

iOS uses the wrong AirPod as the microphone

Settings › Bluetooth › ⓘ next to AirPods › Microphone › Always Left / Always Right – choose the side you hold on the chest.

It whistles

Sound is coming from the speaker – turn on “Mute without headphones” in Settings or connect headphones.

Heart rate shows double or half

The detector sometimes counts both S1 and S2 as beats. The app cross-checks with autocorrelation; the Heart filter, firmer contact at the apex (M) and 10 s of stillness help. The value is indicative.

Apple Watch does not send heart rate

Tap “Connect Health” and allow reading heart rate and ECG. Then “Start measurement on Watch” – the watch app launches. Without a measurement the watch only records heart rate every few minutes.

Is it a medical device?

No. Ausculta is an educational aid; it does not diagnose and does not replace a doctor. Findings such as an “irregularly irregular” rhythm must be confirmed by ECG.

Where are my recordings?

In the Recordings tab, physically in the app folder (Files › On My iPhone › Ausculta). Share them with the Share WAV icon.

Best sound

iPhone probe + any headphones. The iPhone microphone captures the full 48 kHz band without voice processing.

Where to start

Heart filter, the cardiac apex (5th intercostal space, midclavicular line), 10 s of stillness. Then the lung bases on the back.
